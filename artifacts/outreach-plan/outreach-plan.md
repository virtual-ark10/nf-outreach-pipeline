# NewsletterFIT — Sponsor Outreach Plan
Lead-gen lead operating plan — generated 2026-08-26

## 1. Strategic diagnosis

The current pipeline produces one generic template for all 332 leads. Every
email says the same thing: "Spotted SPONSOR in PUBLICATION... I'm building
NewsletterFIT... here are similar pubs... want the details?"

That is the single biggest conversion killer. It reads as a mass blast, not
a personalized note, because it is a mass blast. It never proves we read the
prospect's actual ad, never uses their own language, never gives a reason
THIS sponsor should care THIS week, and every competitor sending the identical
template dilutes the "I did my homework" signal to zero.

We have an asset nobody else has: the corpus contains the sponsors' real,
published ad copy. We can quote their exact words, cite their exact placement,
and match them to lookalikes with a value prop tied to their campaign. That is
the difference between one more cold email and a note that stops a CMO
mid-scroll.

## 2. Segmentation (don't treat 332 leads as one list)

Tier = how warm + how much budget they prove. This drives template, CTA, and
cadence, NOT score alone.

  TIER 1 — PROVEN BUDGET, WARM (repeat sponsors, multiple placements)
  They buy again and again. Money is real, contact is findable. Goal is a
  reply + a discovery call, not a "would you like details" question.
  Examples: Tracksuit (13 placements), Brex (12), Granola (7/6 pubs),
  MongoDB (6), HubSpot (6), Imagine (6 & NEW today), Incogni (4/3),
  Render (7). Use high-repeat hook + real ad copy + a specific next step.

  TIER 2 — ONE PLACEMENT, FRESH ACTION (single placement, active in <30d)
  Proved intent this month. Define-the-category angle. Push the momentum
  angle ("audience growing, similar to what you already bought").
  Examples: Relay (54, NEW today), Livelong Women's Health Summit (NEW today),
  Firecrawl, Cyber, Xplor Pay, Newshook, T-Mobile.

  TIER 3 — ONE PLACEMENT, STALE (single placement, >30d)
  Lowest priority. Light touch, one email, batched cadence. Examples: most of
  the 72-score all-today generic list. Do not burn a quality email here.

  TIER 4 — STRATEGIC/HARD (enterprise: AWS, Salesforce, Google, Uber, Volvo,
  American Express). Separate ABM-style treatment, multiple stakeholders,
  longer horizon. Skip the template entirely.

## 3. The personalization playbook (the conversion engine)

Three layers, stacked. Every email should have all three where the corpus
supports it.

  LAYER 1 — QUOTE THEIR ACTUAL AD COPY (highest-leverage, rarest signal)
  Pull the sponsor's real placement text from articleraws.body_html. Opening
  with their own positioning proves we actually read their ad and instantly
  separates us from every template sender. Verified working in corpus:
  Tracksuit, Brex, Granola, LMNT, HubSpot, Incogni, Render, MongoDB, Ahrefs,
  Attio, Notion. (Glean, DeleteMe have no stored copy today — fall back to
  Layer 2.)

  LAYER 2 — CITE THE EXACT PLACEMENT + WHAT IT TARGETS
  Publication name, article title, category, subscriber count. Use their
  observed topics as the relevance bridge (e.g. Tracksuit sponsors marketing
  strategy pubs; Brex sponsors founder/AI-infra pubs; LMNT sponsors health).

  LAYER 3 — MATCHED LOOKALIKES WITH A REASON, NOT A LIST
  Don't dump 3 pubs. Lead with the single best recommendation (top similarity
  + momentum), say WHY it matches (same category + overlapping topic +
  subscriber range + active), and give one specific momentum datapoint if it
  exists (+42% for Supercompanies, etc).

  The CTA by tier:
  - Tier 1: "I put together the 5 pubs closest to where you already advertise.
    Open to a 10-min walkthrough?" (book a call)
  - Tier 2: "Since you're active in this space, thought you'd want to see the
    3 highest-momentum lookalikes before you buy again." (soft, helpful)
  - Tier 3: single short email, one ask.
  - Tier 4: ABM note to the platform/partnerships team.

## 4. Cadence (quality email, then disciplined follow-up)

Seq 1 (Day 0): personalized email, 110-150 words, one ask.
Seq 2 (Day 4): same thread, new angle — add the NEXT-best recommendation we
  didn't show + one new momentum datapoint.
Seq 3 (Day 8): social/permission touch — "saw you're active in X category,
  happy to share the sheet, no call needed" with the recommendation table
  attachment. Soft close, gives value if they won't book.
Then stop. 3-4 touches max per tier before re-queueing.

does all the differentiation work.

## 7. Metrics & iteration

Track per template: open rate, reply rate, booked-call rate, per-tier reply
rate. Iterate on the OPENING (Layer 1 ad-copy quote) first — if opens stay
flat, the subject/from is the problem, not the body. Expect the Layer-1 quote
emails to out-open the generic template materially; that's the proof measure.

## 8. Corpus access note (how this is built, resopsibly)

Sponsor copy pulled read-only from articlementions (type=sponsor → articleId)
joined to articleraws.body_html, cleaned to plain text. No writes, public data
only. Sources not yet stored in corpus (Glean, DeleteMe, others with no
body_html) fall back to placement + lookalike layers.
